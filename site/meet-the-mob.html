Your chat button lives in the corner of your website, in front of your customers. So you choose what it looks like: one of the mob, or something plain.

Why you get a choice

Most chat buttons look the same.
Yours doesn’t have to.

Nearly every website has the same grey bubble or little robot in the corner, and people have learnt what those mean. Yours is a person behind the counter, so it should look like one of yours.

It looks like your business

A vet might pick Wally the wombat. A café might pick Kiki the kookaburra. Your button should feel like part of your shop, not someone else’s software.

It says g’day, not “beep”

A friendly local face says “ask us anything”, not “you’re talking to a robot”. Customers who are wary of chatbots feel the difference.

Customers can find it

A face in the corner is easier to spot than one more grey bubble, so customers know where to ask.

The answers stay yours

Whoever you pick, the words are still yours, exactly as you signed them off. Change the button any time and nothing else changes.

The mob

Eight Aussie locals.

Each one gives your corner a friendly face and a bit of personality.

Or keep it simple

Not every business wants a wombat.

An accountant, a dentist or a law firm might want something quieter. Pick a plain button instead. Same chat, same answers, a simpler badge.

Pick one when you set up. Change it any time.
